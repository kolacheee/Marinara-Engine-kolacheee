**4. Where I disagree with BRIEF-17**

- **“Taller than any 5-storey stack” is the wrong blanket rule for D.** A bell tower can dominate; a market hall or fountain can be memorable through shape, open space and activity. Making every landmark enormous would lose the intimate scale. The listed 4×4-cell guildhall sprite is also shorter than five 48 px facade courses before their roof is added. Specify where height matters instead of demanding it of the whole set.
- **One roof colour per block is too rigid.** Calm the palette, yes, but allow a repaired roof, an older brown-tiled wing or one distinctive slate-roofed inn. Whole blocks should not become bigger colour-coded repetitions of the same modules.
- **Do not turn “no bare ground inside a block” into “hide every courtyard.”** Remove accidental paved voids, but retain deliberately visible yards and passage mouths. Otherwise C's washing, stairs and back doors disappear from the player's experience.
- **Six to eight stalls “in rows” risks rebuilding the stripes at ground level.** Unequal clusters around an offset focal point would make better browsing spaces. Preserve useful open space instead of filling every gap.
- **A half-cell lane needs a walking check.** At 24 px it may fit one character but become unreadable under tall houses or awkward around another person. Use 48 px for the discovery route and reserve narrower gaps for places whose passage and visibility have actually been demonstrated.
- **Do not postpone street planning and life until E.** A prettier facade sheet can still produce this same static square. Judge an early combined sample with a landmark, an enterable-looking shop, an arch, a turning route and people doing specific things. Also avoid mechanically giving every district its mandatory wedge block and bend; that can become another repeated pattern.

Summary: Give me a place to head toward, shops I can recognise and a small surprise behind the street; more windows will not make this city feel alive.
