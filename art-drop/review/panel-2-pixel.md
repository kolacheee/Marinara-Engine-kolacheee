**Panel 2 — pixel art / art direction**

Reviewed the latest complete `critic-r17E-4` block, market and height renders, the corresponding `r17-E-critic-*` renders, `critic-r17D-3` block/market renders and enlarged crops, A/B galleries and neighbour tests, and C atlases/pilot. Compared native-size views and 3x/4x views against `r15-city-mockup-vs-today.png` and the fantasy house/hall crops. The r15 and r17 house/hall reference files are identical. Coordinates below refer to the named 1x image, measured from its top-left. This is a visual review, not a collision test.

**1. What works**

- E's paving is substantially quieter than D's gridded paving. Keep that reduction in contrast.
- The cathedral has an actual receding body, buttresses and a recognisable silhouette. The guildhall's arcade and clock make its purpose legible. Round 15 lacked these anchors.
- Larger shop openings, projecting sills and the oriel are useful pieces. The restricted plaster colours are easier to read than round 15's yellow/blue patchwork.

**2. Problems, most important first**

1. **The new kit still does not look like the town houses' neighbour.** Where: `r17-A-sample-neighbour-4x.png`, `r17-B-sample-neighbour-1x.png`; the cream upper floors throughout `critic-r17E-4-market-1x.png`. The references model timber, stone and plaster with clustered intermediate tones, irregular edges and deep contact shadows. The new walls are large, flat cream rectangles containing sharply boxed windows. Sills exist, but the wall around them has almost no thickness. The blue-grey panes and repeated diagonal reflection pixels look like graphic symbols. The A comparison makes the mismatch particularly obvious: the existing house feels built; the new building feels assembled from framed panels. Muting colours alone has not solved the cardboard.

2. **Roof depth has become roof acreage, without enough believable roof structure.** Where: E L-block, north-west row `(0–336, 0–96)` and south-west L roof `(0–336, 240–480)`; `r17-B-join-kit-c0-1x.png`; C atlas 07. The north row reads as one orange rectangle sliced by vertical seams. The L join reads as a huge continuous tiled trough/ribbon over several unrelated shops. Tall narrow gables start at a perfectly horizontal rectangular top, then run down to a tiny triangle: they look like roof-patterned cards. The reference roofs have projecting eaves, a shaped rear/ridge silhouette and convincing changes of plane. Replacing the visible ground with orange texture is not the same as building volume.

3. **The market is an asset demonstration spread over a lawn, not a dense city square.** Where: `critic-r17E-4-market-1x.png`, grass between the wall and guildhall, isolated houses east of the blue road, and the enormous paved area around the six stalls near `(600–792, 1488–1728)`. E fills more of D's empty scene, but the wall, civic buildings, shop strips and stalls remain separate exhibits. The stalls occupy a tiny fraction of the court; the northern shop row does not enclose the southern market. Round 15's right-hand mockup was flat, but its continuous street edges communicated urban enclosure more strongly. The new landmark inventory does not compensate for losing that enclosure.

4. **“Side” buildings still face the camera, and the yards are prop shelves.** Where: E L-block's lower-right buildings `(384–768, 288–480)`, C atlases 06/07, and the lower pair in E's height test. The side wall is generally another broad frontal panel with a thin shaded return. It does not convincingly extend down the north–south street. The L-block yards line up a well, laundry rack, tree and shed at regular intervals, with the same collection appearing again opposite. Fence segments advertise the 48 px cells. These are decorative strips between roofs, not cramped rear plots with a reason for each object and an obvious way in.

5. **The trade signs are absurdly large.** Where: E market's north shop row `(528–960, 768–864)`, southern trade row `(528–960, 1296–1392)`, and `critic-r17E-4-market-north-repetition.png`. The loaf, boot and anvil occupy most of a ground-floor bay, hang almost to the threshold, and their brackets intrude into the upper windows. They read as interface badges pasted onto buildings. At 1x I can name the trades, but I can barely read the shop behind each sign. Recognition has been bought by destroying architectural scale.

6. **Repetition survives the variants because the structure repeats.** Where: E height test's lower stacks, the blue-roof towers beside the market's main road, and the two red-roof trade groups north/south of the court. The same two windows, projecting bay, paired windows and door recur in the same 48 px rhythm. Changing a sign, shutter colour or roof texture does not disguise the repeated elevation. Every horizontal course becomes a stripe; adjacent wall outlines become rails. The single-colour roof bands reinforce that stripe at block scale.

7. **The light direction is broadly present in individual pieces, but the cast shadows expose the assembly.** Where: E height test beneath all four buildings; E market beneath guildhall, fountain, stalls and trade rows. Left roof planes are usually lighter and right returns darker, which is correct. But many ground shadows are identical rectangular bars directly below the asset, ending on its width or cell boundary. The cathedral spire has a much more directional silhouette shadow while a whole multi-storey house gets a shallow mat. There is no convincing shared relationship between height, projection and shadow length. At 1x these bars make the objects look raised off the map on rectangular bases.

8. **Outline weight and detail density change between neighbouring pieces.** Where: C atlas 07's densely scratched roof beside its almost empty side-storey; E L-block's clean triangular dead-end roof at `(576–672, 192–288)` beside the noisy rounded tiles immediately left/below; A gallery 12's enormous plain jars versus the busy arched passageways in the L-block. Roof tile boundaries receive stronger, more continuous dark accents than some building silhouettes. Framing every floor with several parallel dark/light bands is much heavier than the references' selective material edges. Some surfaces are nearly blank, some contain a dark mark on almost every tile. At 1x the roof texture wins over the entrance.

9. **The landmarks belong to a different material treatment.** Where: cathedral and guildhall in both D3 and E4 market renders; `critic-r17D-3-piece-guildhall-4x-1.png`; compare `fantasy-hall-0`, `-1` and `-4`. The new civic stone is strongly ochre/gold, with isolated cream-white chips and broad flat shadow faces. The reference masonry is more restrained grey-brown, with rounded block masses and connected shading. Guildhall tracery and bracing form a busy alternating light/dark mesh. Keep the silhouettes, but repaint the materials; scaling these sprites up has not made them stylistically integrated.

10. **The poor district repeats the same failure in straw.** Where: `critic-r17E-4-district-poor-1x.png`, especially the repeated roofs along the upper and lower streets. The thatch is laid out as thick horizontal scalloped bands on rectangular caps. Compare the irregular, tapered, hanging straw edges in `fantasy-house-0` and `-3`. Patches change the surface, not the silhouette. The unchanged tidy shop bases and broad grass gaps make this read as recoloured suburban houses rather than a cramped poor quarter.

**3. Top six concrete requests**

All dimensions below are native pixels; a cell is 48 px. These are drawing targets, not instructions to resize the existing sprites.

1. **A — Draw one convincing 96 px-wide, two-storey corner house before making more variants.** Use a 96×48 ground floor and 96×48 upper floor, with a visible 12–16 px shaded return on the exposed right edge. Give the ground floor a 20–24 px-wide doorway, a 3 px-deep stone step and one shop opening. Give the upper floor two roughly 18×24 px window assemblies, including shutters; recess the glass behind a dark top/right reveal and give each sill a 2 px top face and 1–2 px underside. Project one upper bay 4 px beyond the ground wall, with a continuous shadow beneath it. Sample the reference house's timber/plaster/stone ramps. Use a dark silhouette edge, quieter internal edges and small connected shade clusters at beam joints; remove the decorative border around each whole floor. Show this complete house directly beside `fantasy-house-2` at 1x.

2. **B — Replace the roof carpet with three coherent roof volumes.** Draw 96×96 gable and hip caps plus a 48×96 narrow cap. Each needs a shaped ridge/back edge, 3–5 px eave projection, visible eave thickness and a clearly darker right plane. Use a 144 px-deep cap only when the building underneath actually needs that depth. Make tile clusters approximately 6–10 px across near the eave, tighter toward the ridge; merge some dark tile seams so the entire plane is not a net. For the L-block's 336 px-wide southern run, give three separate building masses different ridge heights and one 48 px cross-gable; join them with an actual valley and one masonry party-wall termination. Add only one chimney and one dormer across that test run. The outline and plane breaks must distinguish the houses before texture is added.

3. **C — Draw a proper lane-facing return and one inhabited rear court.** Build a matched 96×48 side-wall course and 96×96 roof return whose corner, windows and eaves share the same receding direction; make east/west versions with lighting redrawn. Give the outer face one small 12–16 px window, a drainpipe, and a 20 px rear door at ground level. In a 96×96 court, put one 48×48 lean-to against a wall, a woodpile tucked beneath the stair, and one laundry line attached to two believable supports. Leave a 48 px entrance route and draw an open gate. Replace the evenly spaced well/rack/tree/shed parade. One shared well can serve multiple plots. The result should show where someone walks from rear door to lane without reading a plan.

4. **D — Draw a compact market composition and repaint the civic stone together.** Start with a roughly 10×8-cell paved court (480×384 px), cutting a 2×2-cell notch from one corner. Put the cathedral on its west edge with a small enclosed churchyard, the guildhall on the north edge, and continuous two/three-storey shop frontage along most of the east and south edges. Group the six 48×48 stalls in a 3-by-2 arrangement with a 48 px aisle between rows; place the 96×96 fountain off-centre with at least 48 px clearance. Keep a 96 px gate approach entering from one corner and one 48 px side-lane exit. Use the reference hall's grey-brown stone ramp on cathedral, guildhall, fountain and cross; reserve pale highlights for top faces and ledges. Simplify the guildhall to three broad window groups with a dark arcade beneath, rather than outlining every ornament equally. This tests landmarks as part of a city.

5. **New — Redraw E's signs at architectural scale.** Keep the readable silhouettes, but make ordinary sign boards 20–28 px wide and 20–28 px high, with a 12–16 px bracket and an icon occupying most of the board. Allow one exceptional 32 px inn sign. Mount them beside the doorway or above the shop counter, with their bottom edge above the door's midpoint and no bracket across an upper window. Give each board a 1–2 px side/underside and a small cast shadow. The loaf needs three cuts; the boot needs a clear heel; the bottle needs a neck and stopper. Those shapes should carry recognition without a 48–64 px billboard. Put four trades together at 1x with doors and a person visible.

6. **B, with C/D integration — Draw one shared shadow and edge treatment.** Make a small reference scene containing the corner house, its lower neighbour, a stall and the fountain. Draw all cast shadows down/right from the same top-left light: as a starting test, about 8 px east/12 px south for a low stall and 16 px east/24 px south for a two-storey house, then tune by eye to the pack. Add a tight 1–2 px contact shadow at plinths and posts; make eave and jetty shadows follow their actual projections. Cut the guildhall shadow around the arcade openings and connect the chimney/dormer shadows to the roof. Remove the generic rectangular mats. Let shadows continue over paving and adjoining lots when the object casts them there; keep the principal walking route legible through value, not by chopping the shadow off.

**4. Disagreements with BRIEF-17**

- **Fixed course rows must be a connection rule, not a visible border on every storey.** Requiring every floor to stack with every other encourages the repeated frame that currently produces stripes. Restrict some combinations and supply transition pieces. A believable elevation is more valuable than universal interchangeability.
- **A 48 px grid does not require a grid-shaped silhouette.** Keep 96/144 px roof canvases if needed, but allow transparent space, stepped rear edges and projecting eaves inside them. A compulsory fully painted rectangle is part of the cardboard problem.
- **“One-pixel dark outline on the top and sides of every building” is too literal for this reference.** The pack uses material-coloured edges and shaded thickness. Separate touching masses with contact shade, coping or a value change; do not double-outline every party wall or ring every internal panel.
- **“Taller than any 5-storey stack” is wrong as a blanket landmark rule.** A church spire or bell tower can dominate height; an open market hall, fountain and market cross should not. The listed 4×4 guildhall and 4×3 market-hall sizes also cannot literally exceed a five-storey wall plus a 2–3-cell roof without changing the specification. Require a dominant local silhouette and a clear approach, not indiscriminate vertical stretching.
- **“Overlays never cross a lot edge” contradicts useful shadows and washing lines across lanes.** It is directly encouraging clipped shadows. Give these overlays explicit attachment/occlusion rules and allow them to cross boundaries.
- **The sign-size and recognition requirements need an architectural limit.** “Twice round 15” is not an art direction target. A readable symbol cannot be allowed to replace the entire shopfront.
- **“At most two identical pieces in five” does not prevent copy-pasted buildings.** Judge the repeated arrangement of windows, doors, courses and roof shapes. Conversely, one roof colour per whole block is too rigid: keep one dominant family, but allow restrained neighbouring tile values, repairs and a rare slate roof without turning the district into a rainbow.
- **Approve a complete building and a complete corner before separate A/B/C sign-offs.** Their perspective, shadow and outline problems are coupled. A clean seam sheet can pass while the assembled street still fails the owner's request.

**Summary:** Round 17 has recognisable landmarks and quieter paving, but it still reads as framed facade tiles, roof carpets and oversized badges spread across an underbuilt town; fix the building volumes and street enclosure before adding more assets.
