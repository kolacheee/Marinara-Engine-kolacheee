**1. What works**

- Joined frontages and variable heights establish greater density than today's detached houses. The central arch at approximately (552, 310) reads as a passage immediately.
- Red tile, dark timber, cream plaster and green awnings belong in the reference town. Keep that material vocabulary. The current treatment of it needs work.

**2. Problems, most important first**

1. **The buildings read as stacks of decorated cards.** Most obvious on the north square frontage, x384–864/y96–336, and the west block, x0–384/y400–672. Strong horizontal dividers frame each storey; windows, timber and masonry occupy the same shallow plane. Some sills and brackets exist, but they do not establish substantial projection. The reference `fantasy-house-2` also faces front: the solution is not to rotate every façade. Its thick eaves, shadowed wall junctions, substantial beams and stone foot make that front belong to a volume. Round 15 reproduces the decorations without enough of that construction.

2. **The plan is still horizontal strips, even where buildings overlap.** Across the west block and bottom band, shallow parapet caps and upper wall fragments peek over foreground roofs. They read as another layer of frontage, not as a deep urban block. The north–south gaps at x336–384 and x864–960 expose cobbles between cut-off front elevations; they do not show convincing buildings turning along a street. There are roofs, but insufficient continuous roof mass, returns, rear elevations and enclosed yards. Height variation alone cannot fix this.

3. **There is no civic hierarchy.** The market square, x384–864/y336–672, is a large paving sample with small objects scattered across it. The well near (640, 510) cannot anchor it. Tall ordinary houses fill the northern edge, so nothing reads as the institution that owns or defines this space. The wall along the top is an uninterrupted repeat with no visible gate or commanding tower in this view. The player's eye finds bright roof patches, not a destination.

4. **The line weight and detail density do not match the references.** Look at the icy-blue masonry over the north arch, the yellow stone storey around (672, 215), and the broad slate/brown roof surfaces above them. Mortar, window grids, shutter slats, roof joints and trim all receive conspicuous dark lines and bright pinpricks. At 1x this becomes stipple inside a heavy horizontal grid. At 4x the supplied house/hall crops are visibly softer in their source shading, with broad material transitions and fewer equally emphatic internal edges. Do not blur the new sprites; translate those larger masses into controlled pixel clusters. Matching a one-pixel contour specification is not enough to match the pack.

5. **Colour variation is breaking the buildings into unrelated floors.** The north row alternates cream, yellow, cool blue masonry, yellow masonry and timber treatments vertically as well as horizontally. The narrow yellow building around x624–720 is particularly patchwork. Bright straw-yellow walls and pale cyan walls compete with saturated orange roofs. The references are anchored by warm plaster, brown structural timber and substantial dark recesses; their colour changes generally describe materials and connected building parts. Here they advertise interchangeable modules. Dark internal frames also compete with the outer silhouette, while neighbouring façades still merge into a continuous panel grid.

6. **Lighting describes small objects better than the buildings.** There are local highlights consistent with top-left light, but insufficient separation between exposed surfaces, recesses and projecting elements. Under the north frontage at y336, and the east frontage around x960/y480–624, paving begins almost immediately at the building foot without a convincing building-sized cast shadow. The arch has depth because its interior is a large dark mass; ordinary windows and eaves need the same clear value logic at their smaller scale. Additional highlights alone will make this noisier.

7. **Repetition is visible before the details become legible.** The slim blue-window column at x816–864 repeats the same round-braced opening vertically. The green shop awnings around x384–480 and x624–720 repeat the same goods and silhouette across the north frontage, with another occurrence to the east. Small orange dormers recur like badges on broad roofs. Hanging signs sit high in the same decorative band; their trade identities disappear at 1x even when their coloured shapes survive. Changing wall colour does not conceal these repetitions.

8. **The ground and market props have the wrong visual weight.** The cobbles in both source town and mockup are already loud, but covering nearly every gap with them makes the city problem worse. Individual stones approach a person's height. On the square, large slab joints have more visual authority than counters and goods. The existing stalls are roughly 30 px across and read as miniature kiosks beside the huge buildings. At 2x their goods are decipherable; at 1x most are just a striped lid and dark box. More scattered crates or people will not repair this scale relationship.

**3. Top six concrete drawing requests**

1. **A — Draw one convincing 96 px-wide merchant façade before producing the variant set.** Use a 96x48 ground module and a 96x48 upper module. Put a roughly 20x30 recessed door to one side of a 40–48 px shop opening. Give the door a visible 4–6 px-deep step and the shop canopy a 10–14 px projection. Upstairs, use two 16–20 px-wide dark window openings, shutters outside those openings and 3–4 px projecting sills. Use one substantial structural beam instead of several hairline subdivisions. Add a jetty version with a 6–8 px overhang, visible end returns and a 3–4 px underside shadow. Continue supporting posts between floors; make the ground storey stone. Test it beside `fantasy-house-2` and `fantasy-hall-3`. It must look substantial with the flowers, signs and goods hidden.

2. **B — Replace roof lids with complete roof volumes.** Start with a 96x96 roof canvas for a two-cell frontage, then a 96x144 deep version. Draw a ridge, a readable far/back extent, eave thickness and a substantial shaded plane; provide both a front-gabled cap and a roof whose ridge runs along the frontage. Let the eave project visually by about 4 px, with an appropriate receiver beneath it. Use broad tile clusters, with quieter joints away from the eave. Add a roughly 20x24 dormer and an 8–12 px-wide chimney as optional accents, not one mandatory dormer per roof. At the north row's height changes, show a shaded party-wall face with coping above the lower neighbour; do not expose an unexplained strip of another decorated front. Approve the roof attached to request 1, never by itself.

3. **C — Draw one actual corner block with a visible inside.** Use an 8x6-cell test area: two adjoining two-cell-wide street fronts on the south edge, a building turning up the east edge, and a lower rear range facing the northern lane. Give the east-facing side a 96 or 144 px-deep gable/side treatment with sparse windows, a door and a return plinth. Join the roof masses with a drawn valley and hip end. Leave one deliberate 48x96 yard pocket with a rear door, fence and woodpile; connect it to the street through the arch or a 48 px passage. Place this treatment where the west block currently forms a flat strip. The yard must read as enclosed space, and the street must visibly continue around the corner. Do not fill every courtyard with roofing merely to pass a density check.

4. **D — Give the square one unmistakable civic building and a coherent market.** Replace the north-centre ordinary frontage, approximately x480–720, with a 240 px-wide hall/church composition derived from `fantasy-hall-0` or `fantasy-hall-1`: a deep tiled main roof, broad stone portal, three large upper windows and an offset bell tower. For a hero meant to exceed five-storey neighbours, reserve up to a 240x432 transparent canvas, with a roughly 48–64 px-wide tower; keep its footprint and visible height separate. Lower the immediately flanking houses to two or three storeys. Around a 96x96 fountain sprite, draw six 48x48 stalls in two short rows, leaving a continuous 96 px approach to the hall. Each stall needs a roughly 36x28 canopy plane, a visible counter and two or three large goods clusters. Give the square an asymmetrical edge rather than a rectangular border of identical fronts.

5. **A/B — Make a material-and-edge pass before adding decorative variants.** Paint a five-building strip using warm cream plaster, umber timber, warm grey stone and one predominantly muted terracotta roof family. Let one connected building keep its wall material through its height; change material at a plausible base, extension or repair. Reserve the coldest blue for slate and dark glazing, then introduce blue plaster as a controlled exception. Use a dark brown outer edge where needed, quieter coloured internal joints, and the deepest values in openings and under projections. On a 96 px storey, keep the area between the two main windows largely uninterrupted. Make three structural frontage arrangements—door left, door right, central passage—and move canopies and roof accents between them. A recolour of the same window/canopy arrangement does not count as a new silhouette.

6. **New, covered partly by E; shadows also belong to B — Redraw the street's value structure and useful signs.** Make a 48x48 cobble test with stones predominantly 6–12 px across and restrained joints; keep larger slabs for the square, but remove their dark diagram-like borders. Add a 2–3 px kerb top and a 3–5 px gutter beside buildings. Test a continuous 8–16 px southeast shadow band beneath the north frontage, widening locally beneath a jetty or tower, plus 2–4 px contact shadows at feet and steps. Preserve the paving pattern inside the shade. Put an 18–24 px-wide hanging trade sign on a visible bracket above each important shop entrance: a solid tankard, loaf or anvil silhouette with very little internal drawing. Keep a 48 px side-street passage visibly clear between shadow, signs and props. Judge this at 1x with a person beside the entrance; the route and doorway must beat the paving texture.

**4. What I disagree with in BRIEF-17**

- **The stated universal outline is too mechanical.** A continuous equally dark one-pixel trace around every building and every shared edge will reinforce the cutout effect. The references use material-coloured edges and broad occlusion shadows. Specify a controlled silhouette and readable shared boundary, with darker shadow-facing edges and quieter lit edges. Do not double-outline party walls.
- **Fixed visible courses at rows 0 and 47 risk preserving the stripe problem.** Keep the modular attachment rule, but do not force the same bright/dark belt to appear across every building at every floor. Allow the visible beam treatment to vary inside the attachment area, and resolve corners and jetties with dedicated pieces. Likewise, increase windows where they need it; a blanket 25% increase will crowd narrow 48 px fronts.
- **The landmark dimensions contradict the height demand.** Five 48 px storeys already total 240 px before a 96–144 px roof. A 240x288 cathedral or 192x192 guildhall cannot exceed that visible stack. A fountain, shrine or open market hall should not try. Reserve taller canvases for selected towers and spires, lower adjacent housing, and make the other landmarks distinct through footprint, roof shape, frontage and placement.
- **Roof canvas depth, lot depth and projection need separating.** The brief asks for two- or three-cell roof caps, one- or two-cell-deep lots, yards and overlays confined to lots. These are not automatically compatible. Decide the visible projection and occupied ground area on the first assembled corner block; otherwise deeper roofs will simply swallow the promised lanes and yards.
- **“Overlays never cross a lot edge” defeats several requested effects.** A street shadow must land outside the building lot. A projecting sign can hang over a street; a washing line spans a lane. Give those elements defined allowed space and keep the walkable route readable. Clipping them at every plot boundary will expose the module grid again.
- **One roof colour and at most two wall colours per block is too rigid as an art rule.** A dominant family is useful; identical treatment across an entire block can recreate the slab. Control saturation and value, and use material changes sparingly at building boundaries. Also, six different canopy colours should not create a rainbow stronger than the landmark.
- **Do not approve A completely before testing B and C, and do not treat four critic rounds as a quality guarantee.** First approve one façade, its roof, one return and the street shadow together beside a reference house. Expand the library only after that works. The revised brief contains many good additions, but adding all of them to the present drawing language would produce a busier version of the same flat city.

**One-line summary:** Build convincing roof-and-wall volumes, a real corner block and one dominant civic silhouette; stop spending the city's contrast budget on floor stripes, tiny ornament and paving joints.
