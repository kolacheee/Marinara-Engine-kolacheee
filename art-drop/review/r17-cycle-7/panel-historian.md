# Historian — cycle 7

## 1. What works

Narrow frontages, shared walls, shops under dwellings, and the northwest well-and-stair court establish some actual properties. The church west of the market, guildhall to its north, and market hall to its south give the centre an institutional structure. Six stalls form a usable market. The gate road survives greyscale; the enclosure has interval towers and visible stair access. Street-front lawns are gone.

## 2. Blocking problems

Coordinates below refer to the 1920 × 1440 final, measured from its top-left corner. Bounds identify areas, not collision measurements.

1. **Too much of the enclosed town is unbuilt space between small building assemblies.** Look at the northwest properties, **x144–672, y264–720**; the northeast properties, **x1392–1824, y336–768**; and the southern band, **x144–1776, y1104–1392**. There are recognisable narrow courts, particularly west of the guildhall, but too many plots terminate in a shallow roof and a tall front, or expose an almost empty fenced rectangle. Their rear wings, workshops and service buildings barely register. Broad shared paving then surrounds these assemblies. The result reads as an exhibition of urban houses inside a wall. It does not yet communicate sustained pressure on land. Burgage character needs the relationship between street frontage, building depth, private strip and rear access to remain legible across a whole block. A fence alone does not establish that relationship. The comparison sheet shows better individual building volume than R15, but still insufficient continuous urban enclosure.

2. **Street rank is clearer in paving than in the buildings defining it.** Compare the pale gate-to-market route, **x1104–1296, y768–1440**, with the broad western crossing, **x96–528, y720–816**, and the routes through the eastern properties, **x1488–1632, y624–1104**. The main street has a clear surface treatment, but ordinary circulation repeatedly opens into similarly generous aprons. The east side street has a useful narrow section; elsewhere north–south routes are largely gaps beside south-facing façades, fences and isolated end pieces. They do not consistently feel like streets addressed by houses. The repeated rectangular assemblies also leave the required bent street and wedge-shaped block unconvincing at town scale. This needs connected street walls, distinct widths and deliberate junctions before it reads as a walkable dense old town.

## 3. Ranked suggestions

1. **Rebuild two complete property groups before adding decoration.** Start with the northwest and northeast areas in blocker 1. Use **48 or 96 px frontage**, a **96–144 px deep building footprint**, then a **48 × 96 px private yard strip** and, on selected lots, a **48 × 48 px rear workshop or shed**. These are plan dimensions, not the height of the projected façade. Combine `side-roof-d2/d3`, `roof-back`, rear walls and roof-fill pieces so the occupied depth is visible. Align fences with actual party boundaries. Give every retained court a rear door, a ground-floor passage, or a **24 px access lane**. Preserve the northwest well court; build around it. Replace redundant paving with attached buildings, not barrels scattered across the same void.

2. **Reserve the circulation network, then build its edges.** Keep a connected **96 px main route** from the south gate near **(1248,1392)** to the square's east edge near **(1152,1008)**. Use **48 px side streets** and **24 px service lanes**. Narrow the excess western crossing and eastern aprons by extending adjoining plots. Along the eastern route around **x1488–1584, y672–1008**, replace two existing end assemblies with **48 × 96 px side-facing house footprints**, using side-ground/storey pieces with doors or shop openings addressing that route. Preserve two continuous property edges wherever the street is meant to feel enclosed.

3. **Make one bend belong to the property pattern.** In the southeast approach, around **(1392,1152)**, project a plot boundary **48 px**, run it for **96 px**, then step it back. Recompose the adjoining houses with corner and hip-end pieces to make a stepped wedge block. Route a **48 px side street** around it and join a **24 px rear lane** at a T. Keep the main route's 96 px clearance. The same boundary should govern roof, wall, yard and street; a paving jog by itself will not do it.

4. **Give the church a precinct and a clearer relationship to the square.** The space immediately north of the church, approximately **x624–816, y672–768**, currently reads chiefly as a cart/service court. Reserve a **3 × 2-cell enclosure** there with a low stone boundary, packed earth, two small grave slabs and a **24 px gate** toward the market. Move the carts to the market hall's service side. Develop the existing east-side church porch into a readable **1 × 1-cell entrance** facing the square. Keep the guildhall's northern position and the fountain's open approach. The present landmark arrangement is worth retaining; its institutional boundaries need definition.

5. **Make the defensive circulation easier to read.** The north stair near **(1008,192)** is useful. Add a consistent wall-walk top-face strip, assembled from **48 × 24 px modules**, connecting that landing to visible tower doors. At the north towers around **x816 and x1104**, roof silhouettes crowd the interior approach: retain the brief's **48 px ground-level wall lane**, and expose enough of its bend around each tower to show continuity. Roof overlap alone does not prove a collision blockage. At the south gate, clarify the dark assembly above the arch as a usable defended platform: give it a **3-cell-wide, half-cell-deep floor**, parapet and visible access from the flanking towers. The tall shafts currently read more strongly than the working gate structure.

6. **Strengthen the difference between peripheral and central housing.** In the southwest and southeast thatched groups, remove one storey from the tallest narrow stacks and replace their **48 px-wide spire caps** with modest pitched or lean-to roofs. Keep these groups predominantly **one or two occupied storeys**, with **48 × 48 px sheds** attached at the rear. Concentrate three-to-five-storey merchant houses on the market and principal route. Vary extensions, frontage widths and roof junctions, while retaining the limit of two identical pieces in any five-piece run. Repeated miniature towers weaken the distinction between ordinary houses and civic landmarks.
