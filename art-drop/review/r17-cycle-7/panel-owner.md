**No. I would not ship this as the walkable city view.** It still reads as a collection of buildings arranged for inspection, not a dense district built around streets. Better than the rejected town is not the acceptance threshold.

## 1. What works

- No lawns along street frontages. The church, clock hall, six legible stalls and recognizable trade signs give the player actual destinations.
- Larger roof planes, recessed windows and cast shadows improve on round 15. The church and gate silhouettes survive greyscale. Keep those gains.

## 2. Blocking problems

1. **The district is too loosely assembled to sell density.** Where: the northwest courts around `x480–672, y432–576`; exposed courts behind the north-centre rows; the eastern block around `x1200–1488, y816–912`; and the broad western junction around `x96–480, y720–816`. Long brown rectangles and repeated fences separate front-facing buildings into display racks. Wide, largely undifferentiated paving surrounds those racks. Today's town scattered houses across lawns; this version still scatters too many assemblies across paving. Round 15's fronts were crude, but its continuous rows enclosed streets more forcefully. This needs building mass and convincing block returns, not another layer of props.

2. **The square is not convincingly framed, and its southern landmark has broken architectural reading.** Where: the civic space between the church, north guildhall and eastern houses, approximately `x528–1200, y672–1200`. The north hall establishes a front. The church's principal portal points south; its smaller east entrance helps, but the church and hall do not form a convincing facing pair. The east edge is separated by a bright road and mostly presents south-facing houses. The southern market hall at `x768–960, y1056–1200` reads as an exposed rear arcade behind a strip of orange roof and a closed front wall: a cutaway or an unfinished building, not a covered market seen from above. The resulting square is a paving patch with attractions around it, rather than an outdoor room defined by landmarks.

3. **The south gate looks patched together.** Where: `x1176–1344, y1056–1440`, especially the central structure above the arch around `x1200–1296, y1250–1344`. Two slender towers flank a flat stone panel and a dark, heavily scratched box. Their materials, depth and connections do not resolve into one gatehouse. The side stair appears attached to the arch assembly without a clear continuous wall-walk landing. At 3x this is a construction problem, not charming weathering. The silhouette says “gate”; the centre says “temporary patch.” This is the player's main arrival and departure landmark, so it cannot remain the least convincing join.

4. **The architecture still mixes incompatible drawing treatments.** Where: northwest corner buildings at `x480–672, y288–720`, the slate pair around `x1392–1584, y336–672`, and the guildhall roof around `x864–1056, y480–528`. Detailed, mottled corner pieces sit beside very clean cream rectangles and repeated heavy window frames. The hall's roof reads as horizontal bands, while nearby roofs use much larger individual tiles. The references establish one coherent relationship between roof, wall, depth and detail; this image switches treatment at module boundaries. Individual roofs have improved, but several side streets still look like south-facing elevations with corner sprites attached. Repeated narrow spires amplify that assembly-kit appearance. I am not claiming a proven two-in-five identical-piece violation; passing that count would not fix this visual repetition.

5. **Ground and shadows expose the compositing instead of supporting the town.** Where: the square at `x768–1104, y720–1056`, the road along its east edge, and the shadows below the north hall and house rows. The square repeats a diagonal tonal wedge inside slab after slab. Large cast shadows terminate as blunt mats, often more visibly geometric than the buildings producing them. The pale gate route is findable, but its hard-edged strip reads separately from the surrounding streets. In greyscale the hall, church and gate remain recognizable; the rest becomes a broad middle-grey field with repetitive dark building fronts. The wall's extensive texture competes with the inhabited streets. There is a hierarchy, but it is not controlled enough for a finished play view.

## 3. Ranked suggestions

1. **Rebuild the blocks before decorating them — addresses blocker 1.** Start with the northwest block in the roughly `384×432 px` envelope `(96,288)-(480,720)`. Give it an L-shaped perimeter using `96–144 px` deep roofs, a `48×96 px` enclosed service court, and actual side/rear elevations. Carry that construction into the long courts around `(480,432)` and the eastern block around `(1200,816)`: replace most exposed brown strips with roofed rear wings; retain only `24–48 px` service gaps where they have an entrance and a purpose. Narrow ordinary streets to `48 px`, reserve `96 px` for the main route, and make one `48 px` frontage jog at the western junction. Keep a clear `48 px` lane inside the wall, measured between visible obstructions. Use masonry and shop thresholds on street frontages; do not fill these gaps with lawns.

2. **Compose one enclosed civic square — addresses blocker 2.** Use the existing `336×336 px` area starting at `(768,720)` as the core, with a `48×48 px` notch to make its outline deliberate. Retain the north hall and west church, but give the church a substantial square-facing east porch, approximately `48×96 px`, with a clear `48 px` approach. Redraw the southern market hall in its `192×144 px` envelope at `(768,1056)`: a complete roof plane above the arcade, visible roof thickness, four structural bays, and an open north-facing trading side. Put a `96×144 px` west-facing inn return into the eastern block near `(1200,816)`, replacing the existing arrangement there, to frame the square across the main road. Keep the six `48×48 px` stalls in two rows with a `48 px` clear aisle. Their counters, the fountain and the church steps must all remain approachable.

3. **Redraw the gate as one assembly — addresses blocker 3.** Replace the central patch with a continuous `96×96 px` gate chamber spanning the towers above the opening. Give it one clear roof or parapet, a visible top plane, and stone courses matching the towers. Retain at least `48 px` of visibly clear passage. Add `48×48 px` wall-walk connection pieces on both sides and a proper top landing for the `48 px`-wide east stair. Draw the contact and cast shadows together, then check the whole assembly against the adjoining wall at 1x and 3x. Preserve the two-tower landmark silhouette; eliminate the dark box pasted across its base.

4. **Make a consistent corner-and-roof family — addresses blocker 4.** Redraw a `96 px`-wide, `96/144 px`-deep house roof, its `48 px` side-wall return, and a `192×96 px` guildhall roof plane against `fantasy-house-2.png` and `fantasy-hall-2.png`. Match their roof pitch, eave depth, material scale and top-left lighting. Use those pieces to replace the conspicuous northwest and northeast joins first. On each run of five lots, vary ridge direction, width and actual building depth; replace some `48 px` needle roofs with `96 px` hips or shared ridges. Retain the maximum of two identical pieces, but judge the resulting silhouette as well. Keep the 1 px outside contour, sharp grid joins and unblurred pixels.

5. **Rebuild paving and shadows as supporting layers — addresses blocker 5.** Replace the square's repeated wedge treatment with four quiet `48×48 px` paving variants whose slab faces stay close in value. Give the `96 px` main road consistent `2–3 px` kerbs and darker gutters, including purpose-made `48 px` corner tiles at its turn toward the gate. Generate building shadows from stepped footprints, with separate short eave/jetty strips and southeast cast shadows at no more than 50% alpha; remove the obvious rectangular mats. Reduce contrast in wall infill and ordinary cobbles while keeping thresholds, landmark edges and the route legible. Recheck the full city in greyscale at 1x: the square's enclosure and the gate approach must read before individual brick texture.
