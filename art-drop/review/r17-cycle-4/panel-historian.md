## 1. What works

- The clocked guildhall, church, market hall, six stalls and water source establish a recognizable civic and commercial centre. The guildhall anchors the square's northern edge; the church marks its western side. Both towers survive greyscale.
- Joined houses, uneven heights and the enclosed western workshop court give some sense of crowded occupation. The low brown-roofed southeastern group begins a useful social distinction.
- The perimeter lane is visible beside the western towers. The gate approach has distinct paving and a bend towards the market. These are useful foundations for circulation.

## 2. Blocking problems

1. **The gate can apparently be bypassed through a hole in the wall.** At approximately **x1312–1344, y1392–1440**, between the gate's right pier and the southern curtain, a darkened strip of paving continues to the image edge. The 3x junction crop shows no masonry closing it. A shadow cannot serve as a defensive boundary. Regardless of collision data, the image depicts an uncontrolled opening beside the controlled entrance. The gateway also needs visible closure hardware: its present arch reads as permanently open masonry.

2. **There is no convincing system of burgage plots.** Most evident in the **northwestern rows, x144–864, y288–672**, and **southern rows, x144–1152, y1056–1344**. Narrow frontages are present, but their rear property structure is largely missing. Northern laundry-and-crate inserts occupy shallow roof notches, without sustained side boundaries or clearly served rear buildings. The southern rows are almost entirely frontage-plus-roof. Roof projection can conceal yards, but enough boundaries, rear ranges and access passages must remain visible to explain ownership and use. At present these are rows of building sprites separated by paving, rather than densely occupied urban blocks.

3. **Street hierarchy does not consistently organize the buildings.** See the **broad western opening around x144–720, y576–768**, the **northeastern cross-street around x1200–1776, y624–720**, and the **eastern court at x1488–1584, y768–1056**. The gate paving is legible, but beyond it the town becomes a common paved surface around mostly south-facing rows. North–south routes have too few convincing side entrances, gable fronts and corner returns. The eastern court ends at a little building and contains a tree, cart and crate, yet its paved mouth does little to distinguish private service access from a through street. Greyscale reinforces this ambiguity. A planned medieval town can be regular; it still needs a readable relationship between public streets, private plots and service access.

## 3. Ranked suggestions

1. **Seal and articulate the gate first — blocker 1.** Add a **48×48 curtain-to-gate connector** in the cell **x1296–1344, y1392–1440**, with the visible masonry fitted against the pier and continuing into the existing eastern curtain. Preserve the arch opening. Add a **96×48 open-gate/portcullis overlay** within the arch: recessed jambs, iron hinges or grooves, and an unmistakable raised grille or open timber leaves. Connect the wall-walk visibly to the gate structure. Then address the southern tower rhythm: the long curtain west of the gate has no intermediate tower, unlike the northern wall. Reserve **2×3-cell tower sprites** near **x432 and x816**, and one near **x1584** east of the gate, adjusting neighbouring plots to retain the **48 px internal lane**. Remove the doubled northwest tower overlap around **x0–144, y0–240** unless a distinct corner structure is deliberately drawn.

2. **Build two complete plot sequences — blocker 2.** Start with the **northwest block, x144–432**, and the **south-central block, x480–720**. Give each house **48 or 96 px of street frontage**, a **96 px deep principal building**, and a rear strip extending another **96–144 px**. Run boundary fences back from the party walls; expose at least one full strip beside a lower neighbour. Place **48×48 sheds** against rear boundaries and a **48 px back lane** along the plot ends. Include one **48 px covered passage** from the public street. Recompose the rows to make room. Another isolated crate behind a roof will not establish a plot.

3. **Assign roads before filling residual paving — blocker 3.** Keep a continuous **96 px main route** from the southern gate, bending around the inn's southwest corner into the square. Give secondary streets **48 px clear width** and rear alleys **24 px**. Continue gutters and kerbs through junctions, with a stronger value distinction between the main route and service surfaces. Along the north–south passage between the northwest rows, use **side-ground, side-storey and side-roof-d2/d3 pieces**, including an actual side doorway. Give the eastern court a **48 px entrance** and move its cart and crate into a **48×48 loading recess**. Reclaim excess paved margins with **48–96 px rear wings**. Use one coherent stepped corner to create the required wedge; arbitrary façade staggering is not enough.

4. **Make the church belong to the square.** Its main portal at approximately **x552–600, y1008–1056** opens towards the southern housing row, while the market lies to its right. Add a **48×48 side porch** on the church's east flank, facing the fountain, with a clear **48 px approach**. Rework the green patch at **x576–720, y672–768** into a connected church enclosure: a **3×2-cell yard**, low masonry boundary, one gate and a worn path to the church. A couple of grave slabs or a devotional cross would explain its purpose. The current bench-and-potted-tree lawn reads as a miniature public park. Keep the guildhall's arcade approach open; the market hall can remain freestanding.

5. **Give trades and wealth a spatial consequence.** Retain the southeastern low roofs, but add **48×48 lean-tos**, patched rear walls and worn yard surfaces there. Put the inn's storage or stable range behind its courtyard entrance, using a **96×48 rear outbuilding**. Give the western smith/workshop court a **48×48 fuel store** and a visible service entrance. These additions would explain why buildings occupy particular plots; signs alone do not make districts.

6. **Break repeated assemblies through use and ownership.** The comparison shows much stronger landmark identity than r15, but long runs still share the same window-and-storey rhythm. Compose five-house runs as **A–B–C–A–D**, retaining the two-wall-colour/one-roof-colour limit. Vary frontage width, rear extension and upper-storey height before adding decorative props. Keep all new pieces on the 48 px system, with the references' roof depth, top-left light, sharp 1 px outlines and square grid edges. Keep vegetation within enclosed plots, away from shop frontages.
