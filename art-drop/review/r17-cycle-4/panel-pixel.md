# Cycle 4 — pixel-art lead

Not ready to ship. The landmarks mostly inhabit the right world; the modular housing still exposes its construction kit.

Coordinates below are approximate `(x, y)` pixels in the 1920×1440 `r17-city-final.png`, measured from the top-left.

## 1. What works

- The church, guildhall, fountain and wall towers have readable volumes, visible top faces and mostly consistent top-left lighting. The church and guildhall establish the west and north edges of the square.
- Roof coverage and the quieter wall colours improve on R15. The comparison sheet makes that improvement clear, but also exposes how much more convincing the original town houses' eaves, window recesses and material transitions remain.
- The export is crisp: the supplied 2× image is an exact nearest-neighbour enlargement of 1×. I found no export blur. Most building footprints retain square corners.

## 2. Blocking problems

1. **The roof kit does not maintain one convincing high 3/4 construction.** Look at the northwest block `(144–432, 288–528)`, especially the roof junction at `(336, 384)`, and the inn block east of the square `(1200–1488, 720–912)`. Long roof strips, small front gables, crossed hip lines and abruptly changing tile scales butt together as rectangular panels. The exposed stone rectangles between different roof heights read as filler patches, not the side of a taller building. Large roof area alone does not establish the town houses' camera. These joins still look like cardboard assembly at 1×.

2. **The upper floors are flat graphic panels, with an inconsistent outline/detail language.** The clearest cases are `(144–240, 432–528)`, `(480–576, 432–528)`, the tailor/scribe row `(1584–1776, 864–1008)`, and the tall square-edge building `(1200–1296, 816–1008)`. Flat cream fills, shutter icons and heavy horizontal bands sit above much more textured shops. Window and beam shadows do not give those panels the reference houses' depth. Four window interiors in the tall building are pixel-identical at 48 px vertical intervals; tiny changes around them do not break the copied read. The bread, boot, scissors and beer signs are legible, but their thick contrasting borders and facade-sized symbols read like interface badges pasted onto buildings. The required 1 px building contour is not a consistent visual standard across this mix.

3. **Roof texture and material scale overwhelm the forms.** Compare the fine noisy red courses at `(336–432, 288–384)` with the large scalloped courses at `(576–672, 288–384)`, then with the market hall `(720–912, 720–816)`. Adjacent roofs appear to use different pixel scales. Near-black joints and bright orange flecks compete across almost every roof. The scalloped rows look beaded and rounded; they lose the flatter, angular tile masses of the house references. At 1× the orange roof field is more assertive than the square's landmarks. This is texture noise, not an enlargement problem.

4. **Cast-shadow direction is broadly right; structural occlusion is missing.** Under the market hall `(732–900, 804–864)`, the pavement stays nearly as bright as the open square while narrow pillar shadows extend southeast. The heavy roof consequently floats over a lit floor. At the south gate `(1190–1305, 1350–1440)`, the opening contains ordinary bright road almost up to its rim: there is little soffit or tunnel darkness to describe wall thickness. Adding an exterior southeast shadow does not fix these missing shaded volumes. Both fail the solidity established by the reference doorways and eaves.

5. **The greyscale hierarchy does not carry the main route.** The church and twin gate spires survive the squint; the guildhall is less dominant than its surrounding roof field. The gate approach `(1104–1392, 1008–1440)` and the square `(720–1200, 672–1056)` share too much value and cobble activity with every side street. The lighter road can be followed when studying it, but does not immediately announce the way out at 1×. The long bright north wall and repeated high-contrast window grids receive disproportionate attention. This fails the brief's combined landmark/gate/main-street squint test even though individual landmarks remain recognizable.

6. **The western court still presents lawn to the street and has a detached boundary.** At `(240–336, 912–1008)`, grass reaches the open frontage between the smith and boot signs. The little fence at roughly `(240–288, 1015–1030)` sits out on the pavement instead of closing that frontage. It reads as a grass slot and loose prop, not a clearly enclosed rear yard. The churchyard is a different, intentional space; this objection concerns the shop court. Resolve the frontage before calling the no-front-lawn rule satisfied.

## 3. Ranked suggestions

1. **Redraw one coherent roof junction before propagating the kit.** Use the northwest block as the test. Make a 96×144 px cap and a 48×144 px companion using the same roof-course scale and the high-view projection of `fantasy-house-2`. Give each roof one readable ridge, coherent slopes and a single near eave; replace crossed or repeated hip intersections with an actual valley. At `(336, 384)`, describe the height change with a 48 px-wide firewall module whose visible area has a lit coping, a shaded side face and a dark contact edge against the lower roof. Keep the footprint on 48 px cells. Check this building beside the reference at native size before replacing other blocks.

2. **Rebuild the common 48×48 px upper-storey unit around depth, then enforce repetition limits.** Use approximately 20–24 px-wide recessed windows, a 1 px dark recess, a 1 px lit sill top and a 2 px shadow below the sill. Separate a structural timber's face from its narrow top highlight; reserve the single-pixel darkest contour for the external silhouette instead of outlining every cream panel equally. On the tall building at `(1200, 816)`, replace the four repeated window motifs with a sequence such as recessed pair / shuttered pair / oriel / recessed pair. Audit five-piece runs horizontally and vertically by visible motif as well as asset ID; no motif should occur more than twice.

3. **Normalize roof pixels and reduce the orange noise.** Establish one tile module, approximately 6–8 px across and 3–4 px per visible course at 1×, with projection changes drawn consistently. Use three principal roof values plus a restrained highlight. Remove isolated bright specks and most dark internal tile outlines; retain the strongest darks at ridges, valleys and eaves. Replace the rounded bead ends on the north-row rear roofs with angular overlapping tile clusters. Keep one roof colour per block, but mute the common red ramp so it does not outrank the guildhall and church.

4. **Draw occlusion into the structures.** Add a hard-edged, roughly 144×36 px shaded ground region beneath the market hall, shaped to its actual footprint, at about 25–35% darkness. Integrate it with the existing pillar shadows without double-dark seams. In the gate's roughly 96 px-wide opening, add a 1 px inner outline, a 4–6 px shaded soffit and a 12–18 px-deep threshold shadow beneath the arch. Leave the lower passage readable. Keep every shadow sharp and directed south/east where it is cast; do not blur it.

5. **Make the exit route read as one ground shape.** Carry a consistent 96 px-wide paving band from the gate centre near `x=1248` north toward `y=1056`, then connect it clearly west into the square around `y=1008`. Give the band a visibly lighter greyscale base than side streets, reduce its joint contrast, and use a restrained 1–2 px gutter at the edges. Remove interrupted kerb fragments at the joins. Recheck the whole greyscale city at native size and as a small thumbnail: the route must be apparent without relying on orange roofs or following individual cobbles.

6. **Scale signs into the architecture.** Keep each sign in a 48×48 px allocation, but reduce the actual boards to about 28–36 px across and their symbols to about 18–24 px. Start with the bread sign `(720, 600)`, beer sign `(1296, 960)` and scissors sign `(1632, 912)`. Replace cream sticker-like rims with a 1 px dark outer contour, a narrow upper-left highlight and a short lower-right thickness edge. Attach the bracket to a beam and show its contact shadow. Preserve recognizability at 1×.

7. **Finish the western court as an enclosed yard.** Replace the front 96×48 px grass strip at `(240, 960)` with worn paving or packed earth. Move the loose 48 px fence from the street onto the court boundary at `y≈1008`, extending the boundary with a second segment and a clear 24 px opening. Keep any grass behind that threshold. Do not repair this by adding more lawn edging or loose props.
