## 1. What works

Basic floor courses stack without obvious open cracks. The gate aperture is clear, and the church and guildhall frame the square and remain identifiable in greyscale. Roof planes generally preserve the bright-left/dark-right lighting direction.

## 2. Blocking problems

1. **The two-in-five rule fails outright.** South curtain, `(192,1392)–(672,1440)`: five consecutive 96×48 sections are pixel-identical, confirmed by comparing the rendered crops. Northeast potter house, `(1488,432)–(1584,576)`: three consecutive 96×48 storeys are also pixel-identical within its five-floor stack. The square's west-facing side elevation at `(1200,816)–(1296,1008)` repeats the same small-window storey four times. Changing building heights does not disguise repeated components. The current `r_repeat_audit` groups lots by equal base row, excludes wall props, and deduplicates repeated assets within each lot; it cannot enforce the stated rule on this scene.

2. **The curtain wall draws across tower bodies.** Northwest bastion, particularly `(96,96)–(144,240)`, and the next tower's right half, `(384,120)–(432,240)`: wall-walk and curtain masonry cover the tower's projecting face/return. The northwest pair also retains a hard vertical cut at approximately `x=48`, including duplicated doors and windows; it reads as two overlapping tower stamps. This is an actual assembly/draw-order defect. In `r_render`, same-base props share the default layer and are then ordered by x, so a wall section starting farther right can paint over a tower. A convincing gate does not excuse broken perimeter joins.

3. **Exposed corners and firewalls still read as front-elevation patches.** Northwest stepped houses, `(240,432)–(432,672)`; north-middle height change, `(576,384)–(624,432)`; east courtyard edges at `x=1488` and `x=1584`, around `y=864–1056`. The large masonry rectangle at `(288,432)–(336,528)` consumes a whole front window bay but does not read as a receding party-wall face. At the street ends, stacked fronts terminate in thin vertical posts beside pavement, without enough visible return to explain their roof depth. Firewalls are present; their projection and connection to the lower roofs are the failure. Against the reference houses and the cathedral's visible side, these assemblies remain flat panels with roofs attached.

4. **Roof meetings expose the asset boundaries.** Northwest rear/front junction, `x=336–432` at `y≈384`: the rear roof ends in a full-width dark eave and immediately switches to the foreground hip roof, with no legible gutter, valley, flashing, or receiving wall. Northwest adjoining caps at `x=240` and `x=336`, and north-middle caps at `x=576`, also meet as straight rectangular cuts. The latter puts broad scalloped tiles beside much finer pitched courses at the same nominal building scale. At 3× these are visibly separate roof drawings butted together; at 1× they become a roof patchwork. Merely eliminating transparent gaps is not a finished roof join.

5. **The craft courtyard's ground and fence do not join its entrance.** `(240,912)–(336,1032)`, between the anvil and boot signs: the lawn reaches the open street frontage, a thin paving strip runs through it without a convincing threshold, and the short fence sits detached in the road below the house baselines. Its ends do not meet the enclosing buildings or another boundary. This breaks both the no-frontage-lawn rule and the visual contract of a courtyard gate. The isolated rail looks like an abandoned placement, not an opening in an enclosure.

## 3. Ranked suggestions

1. **Fix wall ownership and draw priority first (blocker 2).** Within each base row, draw curtain/wall-walk segments before projecting towers, then tower-owned coping and attachments; do not let x order determine this overlap. Apply this at base `y=240`, especially towers starting at `x=48` and `x=336`. Replace the overlapping northwest pair with one deliberately joined **144×240** bastion occupying `(0,0)–(144,240)`: one continuous parapet/deck, joined masonry courses, and an explicit east return. Start its adjacent curtain at `x=144`; connect the **48×144** west-wall segment below it at `y=240`. Keep a single 1 px exterior contour at each visible join.

2. **Audit placed instances, then vary the offending pieces (blocker 1).** Count every floor and wall instance, including repeated uses inside one building. Scan five-piece runs along connected frontages despite stepped base rows, vertical floor stacks, and perimeter chains. Hash the visible component artwork so aliases and changes hidden below a crop do not count as variation. For the south wall, supply at least three visibly distinct **96×48** parapet/walk tops, with matching endpoints, and place an `A–B–C–A–B` run from `x=192`. For the northeast house, replace at least one of the three identical **96×48** visible storeys with a drawn shutter/sill variant. For the square side wall, use three **96×48** arrangements across its four upper floors. Preserve the two-window-family and two-wall-colour limits.

3. **Draw real receiving faces for the stepped blocks (blocker 3).** Make left/right corner and party-wall modules on **48×48** canvases with an **8–16 px** visible return, a lit coping top, a shaded east face, and a 1 px outline. Stack these at the northwest steps and east courtyard ends instead of substituting a full 48 px front bay of brick. Match the return's roof edge to the same projection used by its **96- or 144-px-deep** cap. Fit flashing to the actual lower-roof intersection; do not place one generic horizontal band across every height change. Retain whole window bays and keep entrances outside the return mask.

4. **Author connection pieces for the actual roof pairings (blocker 4).** At `(336,384)`, add a **96×48** junction canvas carrying an explicit rear-eave/front-hip gutter or occlusion edge; remove whichever existing eave would duplicate it. For side-by-side hip/gable joins, provide a **48×48** valley/end adapter with registered ridge, eave, and tile-course endpoints. Use the current `w2` hips, gables, and ridge caps as the first required combinations. Redraw the coarse ridge-cap courses to a compatible physical tile scale rather than resizing a texture. Give each junction one owner so both neighbours cannot draw an outline or flashing strip in the same place.

5. **Reconnect the courtyard to its street (blocker 5).** Replace at least the front **96×48** lawn strip at `(240,960)–(336,1008)` with the surrounding cobble phase or worn yard paving. Move the short fence from base `1032` to base `1008`; attach its western end to the smith-side boundary and retain a **48 px** opening at `x=288–336`. Continue that opening north to the stair-house threshold with the same paving. Use dedicated **48×48** kerb end/corner pieces so the grass, paving, and gate stop at a shared boundary instead of three unrelated lines.

6. **Make the acceptance render catch these failures.** Add one compact proof containing five adjacent wall sections, five connected lots with staggered bases, a five-floor stack, and both orientations of a tall/low roof junction. Include a tower shifted by **48 px** relative to the wall run: that is the case the current final visibly mishandles. Inspect it at 1× and nearest-neighbour 3×, with greyscale alongside. Require both the instance-count audit and visual join review to pass; a clean equal-base-row audit is insufficient.
