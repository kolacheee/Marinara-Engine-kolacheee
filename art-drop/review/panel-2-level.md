# Panel 2 — level design and walkability

Reviewed native-size images and enlarged pixel crops. Primary evidence: `r17-E-critic-L-block-1x.png` and `r17-E-critic-market-1x.png`; these are pixel-identical to `critic-r17E-4-L-block-1x.png` and `critic-r17E-4-market-1x.png`. Compared with `critic-r17D-3-*`, the A entrance/facade sheets, B street/roof sheets, C junction/back-stack/atlas sheets, and `r15-city-mockup-vs-today.png`. The r15 and r17 house/hall references also match. Coordinates below are approximate native-image pixels, measured from the top left. This is a visual review, not a runtime pathfinding test.

## 1. What works

- E's paving is quieter than D's conspicuous brick grid. The lone person in the L-block is visible on it at 1×.
- The church has a recognizable silhouette and a convincing door-to-steps-to-yard-opening approach. The gate's distinct road surface gives the exit a useful identity. The trade symbols are recognizable, although their mounting and scale are wrong.

## 2. Problems, most important first

1. **The market is still a collection of exhibits, not a dense city.** In E's market, the grass between the wall and the northern buildings, the gaps around the guildhall, and the loose edges of the lower square leave buildings standing on isolated patches. The six stalls occupy two tiny strips around y=1490 and y=1690 inside an enormous paved field. D-3 is even emptier; E adds shop rows without establishing a continuous perimeter. The r15 comparison's right-hand square, despite its flat facades, has stronger enclosure and clearer street mouths. Recover that spatial structure. More roof pixels alone do not create urban density.

2. **There is no dependable entrance grammar for clicking buildings.** In E's market, the signed shops around x=530–960, y=780–865 put enormous pictograms beside much smaller, nearly identical doors. The signs obscure shop activity and parts of the facade; the anvil shop in the L-block at x=480–575, y=390–480 has the same problem. On A's entrance sheet, `ground-shop-w1` presents a black opening, `ground-arch-w1` presents a lit tunnel, and `inn-front-w2` combines a door and passage. These need different gameplay meanings, but the render does not explain them consistently. The guildhall's three open arches also offer no clear main interaction destination. A lantern is too small to carry this distinction by itself. Reference `fantasy-hall-3` does it better: the shop counter and the recessed side door are separate, readable objects.

3. **The character scale has barely been tested in the places that matter.** E and D's L-block place one approximately 16×28 px person around (355,330), away from entrances and deep shadows. He reads as a person; he does not establish player-versus-NPC recognition. The large market has no visible human crowd. We cannot judge a vendor behind a canopy, two people passing, a queue beside a door, or a player behind a foreground roof. At this scale the E signs are several character-widths across and compete with people for attention. R15 at least shows townsfolk beside stalls and doors. The old town's crude blue player also demonstrates how much a stable clothing colour helps recognition.

4. **Paving says “somewhere to walk,” but often fails to say where it leads.** In E's L-block, the central north–south street at x=336–383 is legible, but the pocket at x=576–623, y=336–480 ends against a blank wall without a destination cue. The washing line across the central route around y=250 reads like a barrier until inspected closely. In the market, the slim northbound path beside the guildhall ends in grass near (480,575), short of the wall road. Other paved spurs below the wall also stop in grass. These look like unfinished connections. A dead end can be deliberate; it needs a door, courtyard gate, well court, or visible closure. A different paving texture does not substitute for a connected route.

5. **Landmarks exist, but their placement does not yet teach the city.** In E's market the gate is at the extreme upper right, the guildhall around y=520–910, and the church around y=1010–1440. They read individually, but are spread over a 1968 px-tall test image rather than organized around one usable arrival view. The guildhall faces south, away from the church farther south; they do not read as opposing civic fronts. The fountain and market cross are far apart in the lower paving, and the cross nearly merges with the tall blue-roofed row on the right. The greyscale crop confirms that the church holds its identity; the cross is a much weaker orientation point. Tall objects need sightlines and junctions to orient the player.

6. **The stripes have changed form rather than disappeared.** The L-block's upper-left rear row has one uninterrupted roof/wall rhythm; its lower-left L-shaped roof covers several shops with almost no indication of individual premises. A's street sheet is a long repeated window-and-beam grid. B adds depth but often produces tall rectangular roof fields: in `r17-B-sample-neighbour-1x.png`, the new roof dominates a narrow wall while the reference house has a distinct gable, dormer, eaves and entrance mass. C's side building adds a thin return face, but it still reads mainly as a south-facing facade. From the north–south street, the player gets too few side-facing doors or shopfronts. Roof joins alone cannot solve the “flat fronts” complaint.

7. **Back-of-block life is arranged as a prop catalogue, with weak access logic.** In the L-block's gardens at x=95–290, y=285–360 and x=385–765, y=195–290, wells, washing, sheds and tub trees repeat along the fence. The yards lack obvious, consistent gates and paths from a house or street. Small roofed sheds can look perched on the next building's roof because their ground connection is unclear. `r17-C-back-stack-w2-1x.png` repeats the same right-rising stair on three floors without a convincing landing/return connection between flights. A generator that can repeat these sprites still cannot assemble a believable service route.

## 3. Top six concrete requests

All sizes below are native pixels; one cell is 48 px. These are drawing targets, not claims about existing collision dimensions.

1. **Draw one enclosed, populated market block before another asset expansion. — D landmarks/market + C back rows/side streets.** Make a 16×14-cell demonstration with an approximately 8×7-cell usable square (384×336 px), one 2×2-cell corner bite, and buildings enclosing roughly three quarters of its boundary. Put the church on the west edge with a visible approach from the square; give the guildhall a west-facing entrance on the east edge, or arrange its south-facing arcade on the north edge. Keep a 96 px route from the gate-side arrival to the fountain. Place six 48×48 stalls in two groups of three with a 48–72 px customer aisle; give each group a 24–32 px vendor/service strip and a way into it. Use the existing 96×96 fountain off-centre, with at least 48 px of clear paving around it. The generator must reserve the square, entrances and circulation first, then fill the perimeter with joined lots and place stalls in the remaining space.

2. **Draw a door-and-sign kit with three unmistakable access types. — A facades.** On each 48 px ground-floor module, provide an enterable door approximately 18–22 px wide and 28–34 px high, a 24–28 px-wide lit threshold, and a step projecting 4–6 px. Give closed scenery doors a flush base and a shutter/bar; give through-passages a 24–32 px opening with continuous paving and visible light at the far end. Produce left/right door placements and side-facing variants for 96 and 144 px frontages. Reduce trade boards to roughly 24–32 px wide and 24–36 px high, with a short bracket above head height, leaving the door and display visible. Keep the anvil, loaf, bottle and boot silhouettes. For each module, mark the door's foot position, approach direction and a clear 24×32 px standing area. The generator must connect that area to the street and keep props, queues and sign artwork off the threshold.

3. **Draw a street kit whose edges and endpoints explain movement. — C side streets + new street/access sheet, extending brief E.** Show 96 px main streets, 48 px side streets and 24 px single-file lanes in both orientations. Include T-junctions, an elbow, a 96-to-48 taper, a 48-to-24 entrance, a courtyard gateway and a closed court end. Preserve those clear walking widths inside any added kerb/gutter. Use a restrained 2–3 px edge and a 3–4 px gutter; stop the edge cleanly across door approaches. Replace the L-block's unexplained blind pocket with a small gated service court or an explicit closed end. Draw washing lines with tall side posts and a clear overhead span, plus a view of a person passing beneath. The generator needs connected route endpoints, explicit public/private boundaries and prop-clearance zones; it must not treat every exposed grass gap as a street.

4. **Draw a complete corner-to-back building assembly, including its access. — B roofs + C back rows/side streets.** Use an 8×6-cell block containing several joined 1–3-cell-wide houses, 2–4 cells deep, around a small shared court. Include a 96×96 corner return, a north–south-facing shop entrance, a 24 px courtyard passage, rear doors, and one 24 px fence gate. Keep contiguous roofs, but show a ridge change or party-wall break between premises; vary eave height by a storey in selected places rather than inserting grass gaps. Supply roof returns, valleys and a short rear shed roof so the buildings wrap around the court. Draw an exterior stair as a complete 48×96 flight-and-landing assembly, with a matching return flight where needed, rather than repeating disconnected diagonals. The generator must place compatible building assemblies with known front, side, rear, court and doorway positions, not just stack rectangular pictures.

5. **Give each landmark a usable arrival composition. — D landmarks/market.** Draw the cathedral with a 48 px-wide approach through its churchyard gate and a landing at least 48×48 px outside the steps. For the guildhall, distinguish one 24–32 px entrance beneath the arcade and show a clear 48 px route across its front; mark which arches are walk-through. Frame the gate's 96 px road with a recognizable paving band that continues into the first junction. Put the market cross at a T-junction or a square entrance with a full cell of breathing room from neighbouring roofs, not pressed against the blue tower row. Keep the church spire as the primary skyline cue, the guildhall clock as the secondary cue, and the cross as a local cue. The generator must preserve a sightline toward at least one of these from each main approach and keep their entrance landings free.

6. **Draw the same small playable scene with people and occlusion states. — New character/readability acceptance sheet.** Start with the current 16×28 px person; add an adjacent 20×32 px alternative for comparison rather than enlarging the entire cast by assumption. Give the player a stable blue/teal clothing mass, readable head/shoulder silhouette and restrained contrasting outline; give townsfolk two or three broad clothing shapes and different silhouettes. Populate the compact square with one player, six vendors and six customers/passers-by. Show two people passing on a 48 px street, one in a 24 px lane, one at a door, one under a sign, one behind a stall and one behind a foreground roof. Draw the latter both normally and with the intended reveal treatment. Alongside the art, supply a simple annotated footprint/entrance/foreground-mask sheet. The generator needs separate ground occupancy, interaction points and visual overhangs, plus vendor and queue positions that do not occupy the through-route.

## 4. Where I disagree with BRIEF-17

- **“Taller than any 5-storey stack” is the wrong blanket landmark rule.** Apply skyline dominance to selected spires/towers, not every civic building, fountain or market structure. A low market hall can be the memorable centre of a square. The stated 4×4-cell guildhall and 5×6-cell cathedral also need ground-footprint dimensions distinguished from image height; a five-storey facade already uses 240 px before its roof. Enlarging every landmark creates an arms race and hides streets.
- **“Lots are 1–2 cells wide and deep” is too restrictive and conflicts with the deeper roofs.** Keep narrow frontages, but permit deeper parcels, joined rear wings and shared courts. Define ground footprints independently of projected facades and roof artwork. Otherwise density becomes a row of thin towers with decorative yards.
- **“No street width runs more than 8 cells unchanged” makes irregularity mechanical.** A clear gate-to-market artery may stay 96 px wide for longer. Put bends, pinches and widenings at meaningful destinations and junctions. Do not create arbitrary choke points simply to satisfy a counter.
- **“Any bare ground shows inside a block that should be roofs” needs an explicit courtyard exception.** The current open lawns should not survive by accident, but intentional courts, light wells, service alleys and gardens are essential. Judge whether each open area has a boundary, purpose and access.
- **“Overlays never cross a lot edge” conflicts with street shadows, projecting signs and washing lines across lanes.** Allow visual overhangs with an owning building and defined height/clearance. Keep their ground occupancy separate. The draw-order rule must also account for actors and walk-through arches; sorting complete building images by base row does not by itself prove that a player remains visible behind them.
- **Universal stack compatibility should not apply to stairs, entrances and passages.** Fixed seam rows are useful for plain walls. An exterior stair needs connected landings; an arch needs a route behind it; a shop needs a reachable threshold. Approve those as constrained assemblies instead of requiring every piece to work over every other piece.
- **The critic gives too much weight to isolated art checks and too little to actual play views.** A named trade sign and a shadow on every street are not sufficient. Add a populated 1× viewport, visible entry approaches, a connected gate-to-square route, and foreground-occlusion examples to the acceptance sheet. Do not wait until part E to discover that A–D leave nowhere legible to walk.

Summary: Round 17 has better objects, but it needs enclosed streets, explicit entrances and a populated visibility test before it reads as a playable dense city.
