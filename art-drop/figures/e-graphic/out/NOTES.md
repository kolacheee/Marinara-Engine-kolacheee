# Enamel & Ink — town figure sample

The figures use flat three-shade shapes, a closed near-black ink contour, light from the top-left, and chunky heads at approximately 3.4 heads to a 44-pixel body. The visual vocabulary is deliberately small: an open forest cloak and leather chest for the ranger, a square belted tunic for the labourer, a flared robe and broad sash for the mage. Long ears, swept horns, a pointed hood and a three-bead braid carry identity through silhouette. All artwork is native Python/Pillow pixel drawing from ASCII shapes; the sprite pixels have no gradients, dithering, resampled edges or texture noise.

## Delivery and use

Sixteen independent RGBA layer sheets are in the requested subfolders. Every sheet is 128 × 192, comprising 32 × 48 cells; rows are down, left, right, up. Columns are stand, left step, passing, right step. Left/right/up repeat their standing pose, as permitted by SPEC.md. Frame 2 repeats frame 0. Each outfit's plain filename fits the man; use its `@woman` variant on the woman. Other layers are shared.

`lineup.png` includes all six characters at native 1× and 4× on both requested backgrounds. `turn.png` and `walk.png` use 4× pixels. `walk.gif` contains four exact-colour frames, 150 ms each, looping indefinitely. `swatch.png` shows six skin and eight hair variants at 2×. The JSON contains all exact source, fixed and target ramps. Diagnostic images and check reports are in `../scratch/`; code is in `../tools/`.

Rebuild from this folder with Python 3 and Pillow:

```sh
python3 tools/layers.py
python3 tools/compose.py
python3 tools/review.py
python3 tools/check.py
```

`compose.py` reads the delivered sheets, recolours exact source RGB values by shade index, then stacks body → feature → outfit → beard → hair → hood → staff. Fixed ramps are untouched. Right-facing geometry mirrors left-facing geometry, with material runs relit to keep the bright side on screen-left. The staff contains a grip cutout spanning both bodies' mitten footprints. Its lower brass ring is below that cutout.

## Rig decisions and honest weaknesses

- Down-view eyes use the explicit coordinates x=13 and x=18, y=10–11, with both eyes moving down one row during the two step poses. Those coordinates leave **four** intervening columns, despite the brief's prose saying three. The coordinate specification takes precedence here. All eye pixels survive the final composite stack, including under beards and the hood.
- The bare body bbox is 44 pixels high in every cell, ending at row 47. To reconcile that invariant with a one-pixel head bob, the crown contour extends upward one pixel in frames 1 and 3 while the face follows the head anchor down. This small deformation is recorded in `rig.py`; hair covers it in the lineup. Steps lift one foot two pixels and move it outward one pixel while the other foot remains planted. The motion is intentionally spare and a little stiff; there is no cloth follow-through.
- The closed contour is generated by 4-neighbour dilation. At isolated diagonal turns, an inward ink bridge joins the contour without adding diagonal-only exterior pixels. Some corners therefore look heavier than a literal uniform one-pixel line. Mitten and shoe footprints use the allowed small sizing nudges: two-pixel coloured mitten interiors and five-pixel overall shoe widths keep the little forms intact under ink.
- Tiny faces express direction and eye placement, not emotion. Brown eyes on very dark skin have weak colour contrast, particularly at 1×. Their pixels are present and symmetric, but lighter eye colours read more clearly. The hood leaves a very narrow face opening in profile; it preserves the eye but offers little facial expression. Black hair uses the specified violet-black ramp, so it reads plum in bright previews.
- The long hair is intentionally a broad panel and can look rigid from the side or back. The braid beads are graphic blocks rather than detailed interweaving. The lowered ranger hood is only a small folded shadow on the back of the cloak. These are economical silhouette cues, not detailed fabric or hair rendering.
- A single universal headwear layer cannot fit closely around every hairstyle and also reveal every race-feature base. The hood covers crown hair and horn/ear roots; projecting tips remain the important race cue. The requested lineup does not combine a hood with horns. Long grey hair is visible below the hood on character 5, including at the back.
- The staff must be composited last. It consequently covers some of the side-view shoulder/hair mass. The braid was moved outward in profile so its beads remain legible beside the shaft. The shared grip cutout is slightly broader than either body's individual grip, and narrow light/ink gaps around that grip are a compromise of sharing one held-item sheet.
- Base/shadow area ratios are approximate, not uniformly 60–70% / 20–30% in every tiny part. The two-pixel staff has a 50/50 base-shadow split, and small buckles, soles, horn notches and beard edges also deviate. The full per-ramp area measurements are in `../scratch/check-details.txt`. Highlights stay under the checked 12.5% tolerance in every layer; the actual maximum is 8.70%. The required single-pixel left toe glint is the deliberate exception to the usual multi-pixel accent rule.
- The 2×/3× procedural-ground test and blurred squint test support the silhouette reads in these previews. They do not reproduce the engine's real bloom, lighting or tilt-shift shader; no in-engine validation was performed.

## Review and validation

All sixteen layer sheets were opened at native-pixel 4×. Every non-body layer was inspected over both bodies, all sixteen cells; the incorrect-body outfit half of those overlays is a fit comparison, not a supported pairing. All six full character sheets were reviewed at 4×, as were the swatch sprites and the decoded GIF frames. Native 1× and 2× lineups were checked on both backgrounds, alongside the 2×/3× ground and blurred ground previews. Corrections included the side ear placement, beard clearance below the eyes, the hood's closed back, staff-ring placement, foreground side arms, the braid beside the staff, and the cloak behind the profile torso.

The checker inspects every native cell independently, including frame-edge neighbours. It checks allowed per-layer colours, binary alpha, closed ink boundaries, lone pixels with eyes excluded, highlight share, body bbox/foot contact, eye coordinates and symmetry, and six-pixel silhouette distance with the brief's exemptions. It also checks every final character's eye visibility, all fourteen swatch figures, GIF timing and exact decoded GIF pixels. Presentation labels and backgrounds are excluded from sprite palette/outline statistics; the underlying transparent figures are checked instead. `PASS` describes these implemented numeric checks, not a claim that the stylistic weaknesses above disappear.
