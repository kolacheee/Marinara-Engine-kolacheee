## Steam (Brassworks Sky): judged by a Victorian industrial-history buff

All 12 preview images were read (6 desktop/phone backdrop variants are listed below; no phone overcast-night or phone snow were requested). The prompts came from requests-batch1.md, `## steam` section.

### 1. Verdict: 7/10 authenticity

This is a handsome, usable set. The smoke-hazed sepia sky works, the roofline of chimney pots with a glass conservatory dome (a nod to the Crystal Palace) is real period work, and the steam plumes rise as they should. The calm centre is excellent on every variant, and the composition holds across day, night, rain and snow. Its weakness is that it reads as *generic steampunk* more than *Victorian*. The corners are piles of free-floating cogs and copper plumbing, with none of the cast iron, gas-lamp standards or engraved ornament the era actually looked like. The worst break is at night: the "gas lamps" are gauge housings with a **glowing incandescent filament**, which is an electric bulb, not a gas mantle or flame. Gaslight is in the theme's name, so this needs fixing.

### 2. Per image

- **backdrop-day:** KEEP. Lovely haze, balanced corners, calm centre. The blank gauge dials are good.
- **backdrop-night:** TWEAK. Replace the bulb-in-gauge lamps with a proper gas lantern: a glass-paned four-sided lantern on a cast-iron bracket with a soft flame or mantle glow, not a filament. The stars are almost invisible; either strengthen them slightly or drop them.
- **backdrop-overcast-day:** KEEP. The brown smog is convincingly "pea-souper". The heavier plumes are good, and the centre is calm enough.
- **backdrop-overcast-night:** TWEAK. Same lamp fix. The cloud mottling crosses the centre band more than on the other variants; smooth it slightly in x 15-85%.
- **backdrop-snow-day:** KEEP. Snow on the chimney pots and the strong white steam are right. Optionally, the snow on the roofs could be faintly soot-greyed, as real London snow was.
- **backdrop-phone-day:** KEEP. Clean, and the bottom band reads well.
- **backdrop-phone-night:** TWEAK. Same filament-bulb problem on the left lamp.
- **backdrop-phone-overcast-day:** KEEP.
- **topbar-band-day:** TWEAK. The pipe is fine, but the copper collars look like toy blocks with two dots. Make them proper flanged joints with a ring of rivets (4 to 6 rivets), or switch to a riveted iron girder edge with a thin brass beading.
- **topbar-band-night:** TWEAK. Same collar fix. Otherwise a good gaslit tone.
- **wx-gears:** REDO as weather. The sprites are well drawn, but gears drifting through the sky is not weather, and it looks like confetti. Keep them as decoration elsewhere if useful, and replace this weather sheet (see section 4).
- **avatar-char:** KEEP. A fine clockwork movement in a riveted bezel.
- **avatar-user:** KEEP. Aviator goggles are a cliche but on-theme, and well made.

### 3. Missing or wrong

- **Anachronism:** electric filament bulbs in a "gaslight" theme. Use gas lanterns, a bat's-wing flame or a mantle glow.
- **No cast iron at all.** The defining Victorian material is absent: fluted lamp-posts, railway-station arched trusses, spandrels with pierced ornament, wrought-iron railings. Every metal in the set is brass or copper.
- **No engraving-style ornament.** The set lacks acanthus scrolls, cartouches, banded borders and steel-engraving hatching. One engraved filigree flourish in a corner, or on the trim, would anchor the period instantly.
- **Cogs everywhere, mechanism nowhere.** The corner gears connect to nothing. Even a hint of purpose (a beam engine, a governor with flyballs, a pressure gauge on a boiler) reads as real engineering rather than costume.
- **Airships:** good, but the tiny distant one could become a tethered observation balloon or a second hull type for variety. The gondola could carry a period ensign shape (no text).
- **Palette:** strong. It needs some soot-black and oxidised iron-grey to balance all the gold.

### 4. Weather the theme should have

- **Day: soot smuts.** Small dark grey-black flecks with soft edges, drifting slowly downward and sideways. This is the authentic London sky.
- **Day: drifting steam/smoke wisps.** Pale translucent puffs that fade as they rise slowly.
- **Night: chimney embers/sparks.** Tiny orange-amber points that rise and wink out, sparse.
- **Night: gaslit fog.** Large faint amber-tinted haze blobs drifting low across the bottom third.
- **Rain:** the app draws the rain itself. Add occasional **smoky drift** layered over it, and a faint brown tint if the engine allows tinting.
- **Snow:** the app draws the snow; pair it with **steam puffs** and an occasional soot fleck in the snow.
- **Optional whimsy:** a tiny distant airship silhouette crossing very rarely. It belongs as an ambient sprite, not weather.

The four-sprite sheet I would ship is: a soot smut, a steam wisp, an ember spark and a fog puff, replacing the gears.

### 5. Top 3 redesign moves

1. **Fix the gaslight.** Replace the filament bulbs on all night backdrops (desktop night and overcast-night, phone night) with real gas lanterns on cast-iron brackets, using a soft flame or mantle glow and warm halos.
2. **Replace the wx-gears sheet** with soot smuts, steam wisps, embers and fog puffs.
3. **Add period iron and ornament at the edges.** Bring cast-iron lamp-post or station-truss elements, plus one engraved scrollwork flourish, into the corner clusters. Upgrade the topbar collars to flanged, riveted joints (or a riveted girder with brass beading) so the set reads as Victorian industry rather than generic steampunk cogs.
