## Steam (Brassworks Sky): judge report

Persona: steampunk prop maker and convention regular. I've built enough brass-and-leather props to know when someone has just glued gears on.

### 1. Verdict: 7/10

This is a good-looking set. The palette is right: parchment, brass, copper and a touch of verdigris. The skyline is a strong choice, with Victorian chimney pots and a Crystal Palace-style glass dome. The centre is calm on every backdrop, and the night versions read very well. The weak point is the machinery. The corner rigs are pipes and gears stacked for looks, not a machine that could work:
- The gears have no shafts, belts or drive into the pipework.
- The gauges are blank discs with no needle or tick marks.
- In the night versions those gauges turn into **incandescent filament bulbs**. That is Edison-era electric light, not gaslight, so the "gaslight" brief is broken.
- The weather sprite is literally floating gears, the cliché my persona complains about.

The set holds together as one theme across day, night, rain and snow. It needs a "make it plausible" pass, not a redo.

### 2. Per image
- `backdrop-day`: TWEAK. Lovely haze and skyline; centre is clean. Give the gauges a needle, bezel and tick marks (still no numerals). Add a visible arbor/shaft so the big gear drives something, such as a pipe-side flywheel or a belt.
- `backdrop-night`: TWEAK. Excellent readability and warm windows. Replace the filament bulbs with gas lamps: a glass globe or caged lantern on a bracket with a soft mantle glow, not a glowing wire. Keep the gauges as gauges, with a separate lamp beside them.
- `backdrop-overcast-day`: KEEP. Smog, wet copper and heavy plumes all feel right. Optional: darker water streaks running down from the pipe joints.
- `backdrop-overcast-night`: TWEAK. Same bulb-to-gas-lamp fix as night. The wet glints are good.
- `backdrop-snow-day`: KEEP. The snow caps on the gear tops, the pipe elbows and gauge tops are believable, and the white cold-air steam is the best detail in the set. Optional: icicles under pipe joints and frost on the gauge glass.
- `backdrop-phone-day`: TWEAK. Composition is good, and the horizontal bottom pipe works nicely. Same gauge-needle fix.
- `backdrop-phone-night`: TWEAK. Bulb-to-gas-lamp fix.
- `backdrop-phone-overcast-day`: TWEAK. The gauge has lost its glass and face and is now an empty ring. Restore the glass with a wet sheen.
- `topbar-band-day`: TWEAK. The polished pipe reads well, but the collars with two rivets look like belt loops. Use bolted flanges: two discs with 4 to 6 hex bolts showing, ideally at pipe-section breaks.
- `topbar-band-night`: KEEP, once the day band gets the flange fix too (the two must match).
- `wx-gears`: REDO as weather (see section 4). As sprites they are nicely rendered. The blocky copper gear is the weakest; the 16-tooth bronze gear is the best.
- `avatar-char`: TWEAK. Pretty, but the gears overlap without meshing and the arms go nowhere. Model it on a real watch movement: a balance wheel with a hairspring, an escapement, screwed bridges and two or three red jewel bearings. That reads as genuine horology.
- `avatar-user`: KEEP. The goggles are excellent: amber lenses, a stitched leather strap, side vents and hinges. This is what the whole theme should feel like.

### 3. Missing or wrong
- **Wrong era of light:** electric bulbs in a "gaslight" theme (night and overcast night, desktop and phone).
- **Non-functional machinery:** no shafts, bearings, belts, flywheels, pistons or chains. Gears touch pipes and other gears at random. A fan wants to follow the power path, even a stylised one.
- **Dead gauges:** a gauge without a needle reads as a mirror or a porthole. A needle and graduated ticks give pressure without numerals.
- **Only one family of materials:** everything is brass, copper and verdigris. Leather, dark-stained wood, cast iron, rivet plate, glass and red enamel or jewels are missing. The goggles avatar proves leather adds a lot.
- **Airships:** fine for the genre. To make them feel like craft, add mooring lines, trim fins with visible stitching, or a tiny exhaust puff from the propeller engine.
- **Day palette:** the even gold haze is lovely but a little flat and "filtered". A faint cooler band near the top would help depth without adding detail.

### 4. Weather (the app draws rain and snow)
- **Day:** **soot flecks and coal smuts**, small dark irregular flakes drifting slowly downward and sideways. Also **steam wisps**: soft translucent white curls that rise and fade.
- **Night:** **chimney embers and sparks**, tiny amber-orange points that rise, flicker and die. Also **lamp-lit smog motes**: faint warm dust.
- **Rain:** soot-darkened drops are drawn by the app. Add sparse **steam puffs** rising off hot metal.
- **Snow:** the app draws snow. Optionally add a few **ash-grey flakes** mixed in, since industrial snow is dirty.
- **Gears:** gears raining from the sky make no physical sense. If they stay, make them rare, slow and spinning, like debris dropped from an airship, not the main particle.

### 5. Top 3 redesign moves
1. **Replace the filament bulbs with real gas lamps** (globe or caged lantern, mantle glow) on every night and overcast-night backdrop, desktop and phone.
2. **Make the corner machinery plausible:** needles and ticks on the gauges, gears on visible shafts that mesh, one drive element (belt, flywheel or piston rod), and flanged bolted pipe joints. Carry the flange fix into the topbar band.
3. **Replace the gear weather** with soot flecks and steam wisps by day and rising embers by night. Then add leather and wood accents to the corner rigs and turn the char avatar into a jewelled watch movement.
