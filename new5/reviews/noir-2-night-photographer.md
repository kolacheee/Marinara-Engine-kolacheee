## Noir (Sodium Row) judging report

I read all 13 previews and the prompts. This set has no snow-night, phone overcast-night or phone snow backdrops, so the theme is incomplete across weather.

### 1. Verdict: 7/10

The night pair gets the real thing right. A single sodium-orange light source, cones pushing through damp air, long orange streaks on wet asphalt, a lit steam vent and a blank red sign as the only other colour. That palette is close to monochrome, the way real low-pressure sodium flattens everything to amber. The day set is competent but generic "grim industrial". It has fire escapes and water towers, but the street is an empty paved plaza opening onto a misty skyline, so the eye runs to the horizon instead of being trapped in a canyon of brick. Noir is claustrophobic, and this is a wide-open waterfront. The overcast night is pushed too far: the horizon burns like a sunset or a fire instead of a dull sodium sky-glow. All the centres are calm and usable, and on that front the set succeeds.

### 2. Per image

- **backdrop-day: TWEAK.** The street reads as an empty plaza. Add kerbs, a dark gutter line and tram/asphalt seams running toward a vanishing point. Keep the unlit lamp brackets visible on both walls, because by day they are absent, which breaks continuity with night.
- **backdrop-night: KEEP.** This is the hero image. Lamp cones, the steam lit from below and the reflections are all believable. Optionally add a faint red bleed from the sign onto the wet pavement under it.
- **backdrop-overcast-day: TWEAK.** The gutter waterfall is great. The puddles are too faint for "flooded". Add mirror-dark puddles with broken reflections of the fire escapes. The icicle-like drips on the right fire escape read as ice, so make them water.
- **backdrop-overcast-night: TWEAK.** Drop the horizon saturation by about 40%. Sodium sky-glow is a dull amber-brown haze, not orange fire. The clouds should be lit softly from below and uniformly, not glowing at the skyline. The reflections are good.
- **backdrop-snow-day: TWEAK.** The snow is too clean. City slush is grey-brown and soot-flecked, with tyre-churned dark ruts in the street. The centre street is still wet black, which is fine, but rim it with dirty slush, not white bolsters.
- **backdrop-phone-day: KEEP.** Good edge framing and a calm band. Same street-detail note as the desktop day image, but minor.
- **backdrop-phone-night: KEEP.** Strong. The lamps sit just inside the 14% band, which is acceptable.
- **backdrop-phone-overcast-day: KEEP.** The drips on the fire escape again read slightly like ice, which is minor.
- **topbar-band-day: TWEAK.** The girder is too thin and uniform to read as rusty steel at app scale. Increase the rust streak contrast and the rivet highlights.
- **topbar-band-night: TWEAK.** The orange top-edge sodium glint barely shows. Make it a clear 1-2 px warm specular line with a few wet highlights.
- **wx-scraps: TWEAK.** These read as kraft or parchment, not newsprint. Make them greyer, cooler newsprint (#cfcbc0) with damp darkened patches and one soggy, half-translucent scrap.
- **avatar-char: KEEP.** Rain on glass with a sodium bokeh is perfect, the theme in one square.
- **avatar-user: KEEP.** It is a cliché, but a well-lit, worn fedora on target. An optional touch is a curl of cigarette smoke or a matchbook beside it, with no text.

### 3. Missing or wrong

- **Neon spill:** the one red sign is the only neon. Real noir streets have a second colour fighting the sodium, such as a cold cyan or green buzzing tube in a doorway, spilling onto wet brick at the edges.
- **Street furniture:** add a hydrant, a battered trash can or a chain-link fence at the bottom edges, plus a dark doorway and stoop. Also add overhead wires and a laundry line between the buildings. Right now the alley has no human trace.
- **Canyon depth:** a far building or elevated rail should close the vista so it reads as a street, not a harbour view.
- **Missing variants:** snow-night (sodium on snow turns everything a beautiful dirty amber, which is a must), phone overcast-night and phone snow.
- **What clashes:** the overcast-night "inferno" horizon, and the scraps reading as old parchment rather than yesterday's paper.

### 4. Weather

- **Day rain:** short, thin, slanted grey-white streaks plus a few fat drip beads falling from the fire escapes.
- **Night rain:** the same streaks, but some catch sodium light as amber glints. Rain at night is only visible where it crosses a lamp, so mostly dark streaks with a few orange ones.
- **Steam/fog wisps (both day and night):** soft translucent puffs drifting low. Make them white by day and orange-tinted at night.
- **Newspaper scraps (wind, day or overcast):** keep these, with the greyer fix above.
- **Night ambient:** moths or dust motes in the lamp light, as tiny amber specks. Optionally a drifting cigarette ember: a tiny orange point with a grey smoke curl.
- **Snow:** grey, soot-flecked, slightly wet flakes, not crisp white. At night they turn amber where lit.
- **Puddle splash rings:** add these if the app supports bottom-anchored particles.

### 5. Top 3 redesign moves

1. **Make the street a canyon, not a plaza.** Close the vista with a far building, an elevated rail or a bridge silhouette. Add kerbs, a gutter and furniture at the bottom edges. Keep unlit lamp brackets in every day image so day and night match.
2. **Add a second light colour.** Give the theme one cold neon (cyan or green) spilling at an edge in the night images, reflected in the wet street. It would answer the sodium orange the way the red sign starts to. Then calm the overcast-night horizon to a dull amber haze.
3. **Complete the weather set.** Produce snow-night (sodium amber on dirty slush), phone overcast-night and phone snow. Make the day snow grey-brown and churned, not white.
