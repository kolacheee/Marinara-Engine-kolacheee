## Noir (Sodium Row): judge report

All 13 previews checked against the prompts in the `## noir` section. Only these variants exist: no phone overcast-night, no phone snow and no snow-night.

### 1. Verdict: 6/10
The night set is the real thing. Sodium lamps cast orange cones, the wet asphalt throws long orange streaks, the manhole steam glows, and a red sign without lettering burns on the right wall. That reads as noir. The day set reads as a generic post-industrial matte painting. It is a photoreal concept-art render, not the "painterly graphic-novel" the prompt asked for: no hard shadows, no ink, no high contrast. The bigger problem for this persona is that the street is not a street. The buildings sit at the far edges of a huge empty plaza that opens onto a distant skyline, so it looks like a waterfront lot, not a narrow alley where something happens. There is no story in it. Nobody lives here, nothing is lit for a reason, and there is no office, no bar and no case. The palette and materials (brick, rust, fire escapes, water towers) hold the set together well, and the calm centre works in every image.

### 2. Per image
- backdrop-day: TWEAK. The centre is calm, but the empty plaza kills the mood. Narrow the street with a near-to-far perspective so the walls converge, and push contrast toward graphic-novel ink.
- backdrop-night: KEEP. The strongest image. Optional: add venetian-blind slat shadows in one lit upper window on the right building.
- backdrop-overcast-day: TWEAK. The left fire escape grows icicle-like drips, which is wrong for rain; make them water drips. The clouds in the centre are too busy and mid-grey, which hurts both light and dark text. Flatten the centre.
- backdrop-overcast-night: KEEP. Excellent glow on the horizon and in the puddles. Check that cloud texture in the centre stays faint at the final darkness.
- backdrop-snow-day: TWEAK. Snow sits only on curbs and ledges while the street stays rain-wet; add grey slush and tyre ruts so it reads as winter. The sky is the lightest of the set and nearly white, so check dark-text contrast.
- backdrop-phone-day: KEEP. Clean, with a good calm band.
- backdrop-phone-night: KEEP. The lamp cones reach slightly into the calm band at about 20 to 35% height; trim them a little.
- backdrop-phone-overcast-day: TWEAK. Icicles hang from both fire escapes; replace them with water drips.
- topbar-band-day: TWEAK. At bar height it reads as a thin dark line and the rivets almost vanish. Enlarge the rivets and strengthen the rust streaks.
- topbar-band-night: TWEAK. The orange reflection along the top edge is barely visible. Make it a clear thin orange highlight.
- wx-scraps: TWEAK. These look like beige kraft paper or parchment, closer to a treasure map. Newsprint should be greyer, cooler and thinner (#cfcbc0), with yellowed edges. Make one a torn corner and one a folded flat sheet so the four read differently.
- avatar-char: KEEP. A rain-streaked window with a sodium blur is perfect noir mood. The rough blank-wall look fits as the "character".
- avatar-user: KEEP. The worn fedora in raking orange light is iconic and in the same style as avatar-char.

### 3. Missing or wrong
- **Story anchors.** A pulp fan expects one second-floor window, lit with venetian-blind shadows, above a bar with a glowing doorway. Add a "detective's office above the bar" on one edge building. A blank frosted-glass office door pane is also possible.
- **Hard light.** There are no slatted shadows, no deep blacks cut by single light sources, and no wet-poster or matchbook textures. The day images especially lack the chiaroscuro the genre demands.
- **Era cues.** The buildings could be from any decade. Add 1940s details: a bare-bulb fire-exit lamp, a phone box silhouette or newspaper stand at the edge (no text), wooden telephone poles with sagging wires at the edges, a fire hydrant.
- **Clashes.** The icicles in the rain variants. The beige parchment scraps. The overly wide plaza and distant skyline, which make it feel like an apocalypse skyline rather than a cramped city.

### 4. Weather
- **Rain (day and night).** The genre's signature, so it should be heavy, slanted and long. At night, tint some streaks orange where they pass the lamp zones (edges only). Add drips falling from the fire escapes at the edges.
- **Fog or drifting steam (night, overcast).** Low, slow wisps at the bottom, lit orange at night and grey by day.
- **Blowing newsprint (day, wind).** The existing scraps, tumbling and flipping slowly in low diagonal gusts, never too many.
- **Snow (day and night).** Sparse, wet, greyish flakes rather than fluffy white ones, falling a little fast. At night, flakes passing the lamps glint orange.
- **Optional night touch.** One cigarette-smoke curl or a few lamp glints on the drops. Avoid lightning.

### 5. Top 3 redesign moves
1. **Give it a story.** Add a lit, blind-slatted office window above a bar doorway on one edge building (night: warm orange light with slat shadows; day: unlit, blinds half-drawn). This one motif turns a backdrop into a setting.
2. **Make it a narrow street with hard light.** Use a near-to-far perspective so the walls close in, a single lamp per side, real ink-black shadows and graphic-novel contrast. Keep the centre calm, but as darkness or fog rather than open sky over a distant city.
3. **Fix the weather assets.** Remove the icicles from the rain variants, recolour the scraps to grey newsprint, and make the girder trim legible: bigger rivets, rust, and a visible orange edge at night. Then fill the missing variants (phone overcast-night, phone snow, snow-night) so rain, the genre's core weather, is complete on both screens.
